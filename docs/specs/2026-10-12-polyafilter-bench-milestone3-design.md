## 0. Overview

Milestone 3 turns removed reads into lost UMIs, measures what that costs each cell and each cell class, closes the checks that milestone 2 left open, and builds the final report page.

- Stage 23 reads each BAM once more and stores, for every group of reads that share a cell, a UMI and a gene, how many of its reads each setting removes.
- Stage 24 recounts UMIs with Cell Ranger's documented rule before and after filtering and writes the matrices: UMIs lost, gained and moved to another gene.
- Stage 25 gives each cell a coarse class from retinal marker genes.
- Stage 26 measures the loss per cell and per class and tests whether a class stops forming a cluster.
- Stage 27 gives accuracy by cell class and by expression level.
- Stage 28 asks whether molecules with a polyA-trimmed read look like the rest.
- Stage 29 gives the burden by region, corrected for label purity.
- Stage 30 says what kind of long-read molecule ends at each site.
- Stage 31 compares the sites used in nuclei with those used in cells.
- Stage 32 tests which ERCC mix is in the tube.
- Stage 33 (optional) reruns Cell Ranger once on a filtered BAM.
- Stage 34 builds the report page.

You are asked six things (section 5): the marker panel and the class method (D1), the extra settings beyond the ten the spec fixes (D3), how to get isoform context for long reads, your own tecap included (D5), how to close the ERCC mix question (D7), whether to rerun Cell Ranger once (D8), and how to treat ambient RNA and doublets (D9). One item needs only your OK: a caveat sentence on the milestone 2 page (section 7).

## 1. Goal and questions answered

Milestone 2 answered spec Q1 (burden) and Q2 (accuracy) on truth molecules and fixed the settings: default mode c250_l7_m1_e, intron mode c200_l10_m2_i (rule run on 1417 called cells). Milestone 3 answers Q3 (cost), closes the parts of Q1 and Q2 that waited for cell types and for a count of all molecules, and delivers the final report page.

Questions, in the order the report will answer them:

1. **Recount.** Can we rebuild the Cell Ranger filtered matrix from the BAM with the counting rule 10x documents, so that "UMIs after filtering" is our own count with a known error? (spec 8)
2. **Cost per cell.** How many UMIs and genes does each called cell lose at the chosen and published settings, how many UMIs appear or move to another gene because a competing read group was removed, and how does the intronic share change? (spec Q3)
3. **Cost per cell class.** Does one class lose much more than the others? Does any class stop forming a cluster after filtering, as the endothelial type did in the paper's mouse cortex nuclei? (spec 8)
4. **Accuracy by cell class and expression level.** Do sensitivity and specificity of the chosen and published settings depend on the cell class or on how highly a gene is expressed? (spec 7 strata)
5. **Is the truth set representative?** Do molecules with a polyA-trimmed read differ from the other counted molecules by region, gene, read position relative to the annotated 3' end, and cell class? Does the record-level comparison of milestone 2 hold within called cells once duplicates are removed in every region? (spec 4.1, 11)
6. **Burden by region, corrected for label purity**, with intervals (deferred from milestone 2).
7. **Long reads.** When a BD67 or BD70 read ends at an internally primed site, what kind of molecule is it: an otherwise complete isoform cut short in the CDS or 3' UTR, or an unspliced transcript ending in an intron? (spec 4.3)
8. **Nuclei against cells at shared genes and sites.** Site sequence, hexamer and distance to the annotated end. (spec 8)
9. **ERCC mix identity**: what settles it.
10. **One report page**, generated by code from the output files.

Limits that hold for every nuclei against cells statement: tissue (adult retina against organoid), maturity, cell class mix and nucleus against cell are confounded, and 1179 was counted with include-introns false. Those comparisons stay descriptive. The impact analysis is descriptive only (user decision).

### 2.3 What the probe and the checks settled

All of this is on chr21 and chr22 unless a line says genome-wide.

- **Recount by the xf bits.** Among records whose CB is a called cell: unique (CB, UB, GX) over records with a UB, a single-gene GX (no ';'), xf bit 1 set and xf bit 2 unset. Zero difference to the filtered matrix on all chr21 and chr22 genes: 267,082 / 252,484 / 2,329,802 entries compared and equal [P]. An independent recount of 1417 chr22 gives the same: 252,736 molecules, 158,553 of 158,553 entries equal ([V] `rerun_1417_chr22.json`). Bit meanings, Cell Ranger 9.0 BAM page (https://www.10xgenomics.com/support/software/cell-ranger/9.0/analysis/outputs/cr-outputs-bam, read from a Wayback copy, [R] A): "1 - The read is confidently mapped to the transcriptome 2 - This read's barcode, UMI, and feature combination was discarded in favor of a different feature with higher read support ... 8 - This read is representative for a molecule and is treated as a UMI count".
- **Read groups.** A read group is (cell, UMI, gene) over primary called-cell records with a UB, a single-gene GX and xf bit 1 set. In called cells xf takes only the values 0, 17, 19 and 25 [P]. Of 845,254 / 821,099 / 7,977,163 records with xf bit 1, none has a multi-gene GX and none lacks GX; 172,933 / 174,488 / 230,319 carry no UB and belong to no read group. The rest form 470,820 / 459,857 / 5,408,758 read groups: 467,969 / 457,177 / 5,386,179 with bit 2 unset on every read (the counted molecules) and 2,851 / 2,680 / 22,579 with bit 2 set on every read (xf 19). No group mixes the two ([C] `m1_rule_check_<lib>.json`, script `m1_rule_check.py`). The xf 19 groups are 0.61% / 0.59% / 0.42% of counted molecules ([C] `derived_shares.json`).
- **Records without a UB.** Genome-wide, 8,396,895 of 41,426,262 primary called-cell records with xf bit 1 carry no UB in 1417 (20.3%), 8,707,198 of 40,807,320 in 1419 (21.3%) and 9,364,986 of 323,164,931 in 1179 (2.9%) ([V2] `a1b_genomewide_<lib>.json`, record_counts). They belong to no read group and are never counted. The reason is in the source: a UMI is valid only when it has no N, is not a homopolymer and has no base with a quality below 10 (`const UMI_MIN_QV: u8 = 10;` and `let is_valid = !(has_n || is_homopolymer || low_min_qual);`, [CR] `umi/src/info.rs` lines 6 and 25), and the corrected UMI tag is attached to valid UMIs only (`// Attach the corrected UMI tag to the SAM records.` followed by `if self.umi_info.is_valid {`, [CR] `tx_annotation/src/read.rs` lines 548 and 549). Our observation agrees. On 1417, chr22:20,000,000 to 24,000,000, called cells: all 6,880 such records have a base below quality 10 in UY ("Chromium molecular barcode read quality. Phred scores as reported by sequencer.", BAM page, copy in `M3D/research/dl/bam.txt` line 87), and none of the 29,435 records with a UB has one. On 1179, chr22:20,000,000 to 21,000,000: 3,728 of 3,734, the other 6 with an N or a homopolymer in UR ([C] `ub_missing_1417_chr22_20_24Mb.json`, `ub_missing_1179_chr22_20_21Mb.json`, script `ub_missing_check.py`). Cell Ranger's own summary shows the same gap between the runs: Q30 bases in UMI 89.0% / 87.9% / 97.8% (`outs/metrics_summary.csv`). This does not touch the benchmark; it costs the two nuclei libraries a fifth of their confidently mapped reads.
- **The documented counting rule, checked as far as two chromosomes allow.** Rule (Cell Ranger 9.0 algorithm page, https://www.10xgenomics.com/support/software/cell-ranger/9.0/algorithms-overview/cr-gex-algorithm, read from a Wayback copy, [R] A): "If two or more groups of reads have the same barcode and UMI, but different gene annotations, the gene annotation with the most supporting reads is kept for UMI counting, and the other read groups are discarded. In case of a tie for maximal read support, all read groups are discarded". Our observations ([C] `m1_rule_check_<lib>.json`; the counted molecule of each cell and UMI is looked up genome-wide in `molecule_info.h5`):
  - Where the cell and UMI of an xf 19 group has a counted molecule anywhere in the genome, that molecule has strictly more reads than the xf 19 group: 545 of 545, 544 of 544, 4,557 of 4,557. The winner leads by a single read in 416, 422 and 3,331 of them.
  - The other xf 19 groups, 2,306 / 2,136 / 18,022, belong to a cell and UMI with no counted molecule anywhere (the tie case, our reading). 308 / 332 / 2,194 of them (13.4% / 15.5% / 12.2%) have a visible partner group with the same read count. Where two or more groups of such a cell and UMI are visible (154 / 166 / 1,097 cases), the maximum is shared by at least two of them in every case. For the rest only one group lies on chr21 or chr22, so the partner cannot be seen here.
  - The rule applied to the visible groups alone, without bits 2 and 8, never contradicts the matrix on these two chromosomes: no counted group fails to be the strict maximum (0 / 0 / 0; genome-wide see below). It names 2,488 / 2,272 / 19,807 groups that are not counted; for 490 / 468 / 3,979 of them the counted gene sits on another reference with strictly more reads, and for 1,998 / 1,804 / 15,828 the cell and UMI has no counted molecule and the partner is not visible.
  - Genome-wide, no cell and UMI has two counted molecules: 0 of 23,036,919 / 22,704,233 / 219,393,056 rows of pass_filter barcodes in `molecule_info.h5`.
  These four points are the partial check of version 2, on two chromosomes.
- **The same rule genome-wide** ([V2] `a1b_genomewide_<lib>.json`, script `a1b_genomewide.py`: one pass over each BAM with the read groups and the key layout of 3.1. 1417 and 1419 were run twice with identical output; 1179 once, 21 min 57 s and 35.5 GB resident with 6 workers).
  - Read groups: 23,158,211 / 22,822,639 / 220,237,668, from 33,029,367 / 32,100,122 / 313,799,945 records. No record with xf bit 1 has a multi-gene GX, lacks GX, has a GX outside the feature list, or has a UB that is not 12 bases of A, C, G and T. No read group has records on two references, none mixes bit 2, and the largest has 51 / 38 / 100 reads.
  - The xf rule is exact: all 10,381,047 / 10,007,682 / 76,907,650 nonzero entries of the filtered matrix are reproduced and no entry differs.
  - 72,718 / 70,845 / 502,396 cells and UMIs have two or more read groups (0.31% / 0.31% / 0.23% of 23,085,006 / 22,751,340 / 219,731,605). In 48,084 / 47,105 / 338,520 of them the maximum is tied and the rule counts nothing; in 24,634 / 23,740 / 163,876 one gene leads.
  - The documented rule on these groups, without bits 2 and 8, counts 23,036,922 / 22,704,235 / 219,393,085 molecules against Cell Ranger's 23,036,919 / 22,704,233 / 219,393,056. The matrices differ in 5 of 10,381,050, 2 of 10,007,683 and 33 of 76,907,657 entries (0.48, 0.20 and 0.43 per million). Groups counted by the rule and not by Cell Ranger: 4 / 2 / 31; the other way round: 1 / 0 / 2.
  - Cause, from the source: Cell Ranger decides low support while UMI correction is under way. It counts reads per raw UMI and gene (`// Count (raw UMI, feature) pairs to prepare for UMI correction.`, [CR] `tx_annotation/src/mark_dups.rs` line 130), moves a single read of each corrected group to its corrected UMI (`// Before determining low-support UMI-genes, count one read of each corrected UMI` / `// to match the behaviour of Cell Ranger 3.`, lines 226 and 227), and marks the low-support pairs on those counts (`/// Mark as low support umigenes with frequency below the maximum for that UMI.`, line 86). The algorithm page reads as if the groups were formed on corrected UMIs ("Cell Ranger again groups the reads by barcode, UMI (possibly corrected), and gene annotation", `M3D/research/dl/algo.txt` line 104). An emulation of the code from the UR, UB and GX tags reproduces xf bit 2 on every record, 0 of 33,029,367 / 32,100,122 / 313,799,945 differing, and every matrix entry ([V2] `a1d_code_rule_<lib>.json`, script `a1d_code_rule.py`; the 1179 run took 45 min 49 s and 54 GB resident). All 5, 2 and 33 differing cells and UMIs have a corrected read at that UMI.

### 3.1 Stage 23: read-group pass

**Purpose.** One pass per reference that writes one row per read group of a called cell: how many reads it has, how many of them each setting of `IMPACT_SETTINGS` removes, and the fields the later stages need. Alongside it counts records per called cell and region.

**What is counted.** The object is the read group: (cell, UMI, gene) over primary records with a called-cell CB, a UB, a single-gene GX present in `features.tsv.gz` and xf bit 1 set (xf 25, 17 and 19 in the probe, 2.3). Its winner reads are the records with xf bit 2 unset. Version 1 kept only the counted molecules and called one lost when all its supporters were removed. That misses three cases the documented rule produces where two or more genes share a cell and UMI: a tie that the filter breaks (a UMI appears), a winner cut down to a tie or below another gene (a UMI disappears or moves although reads remain), and a winner removed in full (the UMI moves to another gene). Stage 24 applies the rule to the read groups; this stage delivers them.

**Method.** New module `pafbench/molstream.py`, a sibling of `stream.scan_reference`, not a change to it: `scan_reference` is the engine validated record for record against the original tool ([M2] section 11) and stays untouched. The sibling imports the parts that define removal (`coverage_prefix_chunked`, `reference_pieces`, `_pack`) and has its own chunk class, kernel and part writer (3.0). Per record the kernel returns a bit mask with one bit per setting ("this record is removed at setting t"), 32 bits for the 14 settings of D3.

For each record of a read group the pass appends 17 bytes to per-reference arrays:

| Field | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|
| key | uint64 | cell index (24 bits), UMI in 2-bit code (24 bits), gene index (16 bits), in this order, so that sorting brings the groups of one cell and UMI together |
| removed | uint32 | bit t set when the record is removed at setting t |
| flags | uint8 | bit 0 RE E, bit 1 RE N, bit 2 the record has a pa tag above 0, bit 3 xf bit 2 is set |
| end3 | int32 | the record's 3'-most aligned base in transcript sense |

At the end of the reference the arrays are sorted by key and reduced to one row per read group, 51 bytes with 14 settings:

| Field | Type | Content |
|---|---|---|
| key | uint64 | as above |
| n_reads | uint16 | records of the group |
| n_winner_reads | uint16 | records with xf bit 2 unset |
| n_pa | uint16 | records with a pa tag above 0 |
| rm_<tag> | uint16, one per setting | records of the group removed at that setting |
| re_class | uint8 | 1 E only, 2 N only, 3 both |
| end3 | int32 | 3'-most base over all records of the group |
| end3_nonpa | int32 | 3'-most base over the records without a pa tag; -1 when every record has one |

uint16 leaves room: the largest read count of a counted molecule is 100 ([C] `support_per_ref.json`, max_support). The pass stops if a group reaches 65,535 reads. `end3_nonpa` and `n_pa` exist for stage 28: the 3' end of a pa read is the priming site, so `end3` of a labeled molecule and of an unlabeled one are different kinds of position (3.6).

Why a whole-reference table and no streaming flush: records come in position order and the reads of one group can lie far apart (2.3), so no window is safe. The table is small enough (below).

Alongside, per called cell, RE (E, N, I, none) and presence of a GX tag: primary records in total, primary records with a UB, and primary records removed per setting. This is the record-level per-cell total by region that the scope asks for; in 1179 it is the only place where intronic removal shows. The split by GX separates sense records from antisense ones for the record share of 3.4. The same counts are kept over all barcodes per RE, only to tie the pass to stage 13 (check A3).

**A unit without duplicates, defined the same way in every region.** Per called cell, RE and presence of a GX tag, the pass also counts distinct (cell, UMI, strand, 1 kb bin) among primary records with a UB, the bin being the record's leftmost aligned position divided by 1,000. Stage 28 needs it: Cell Ranger sets the SAM duplicate flag only on reads of a counted molecule (2.3; in the source the flag is set inside `if let Some(dup_info)` on reads that are neither the UMI count nor low support, [CR] `tx_annotation/src/read.rs` lines 560 to 568), so the flag cannot remove duplicates among intergenic records, antisense records or the intronic records of 1179. The unit needs no gene. Its limits are printed with it. (a) One molecule gives several units when its reads fall in different bins or RE classes, and spliced molecules spread further (the reads of one counted molecule span more than 1,000 nt for 6.9% of molecules in 1417 and 20.4% in 1179, 2.3), so exonic molecules give more units than unspliced ones. (b) UB is the corrected UMI only on reads that went through duplicate marking and the raw UMI elsewhere ([CR] `read.rs` lines 548 to 556), so regions that are not counted keep uncorrected UMIs. Our observation on chr22:20,000,000 to 24,000,000, called cells: UB differs from UR on 0 of 19,355 xf 0 records in 1417 and 0 of 309,762 in 1179, against 536 of 29,435 and 4,005 of 826,836 records with xf bit 1 ([V2] `ub_vs_ur_by_xf.json`). (c) Records without a UB cannot be placed (79% to 81% of records have a UB in the nuclei libraries and 97% to 98% in 1179, with less than 2 points between RE classes; [C] `derived_shares.json`, with_UB_share_by_RE). To show how far a unit is from a molecule where both are known, the pass counts the same unit a second time over read-group records only (per cell and RE), and the page prints, by RE, units per read group that has a record of that RE. The plain unit totals cannot serve for that ratio: they include antisense records, records with xf 0 and records without a counted gene, none of which is in a read group (on 1417 chr21 and chr22, 672,321 of the 1,170,281 called-cell records with RE E or N are in one; [P] RE_E_called_primary and RE_N_called_primary, [V3] `probe_ruling_1417.json`). Records come in order of leftmost position (our observation: header `SO:coordinate` and no record starting before its predecessor among 231,228 / 213,218 / 2,495,177 records of chr22:20,000,000 to 30,000,000, [C] `sorted_check.json`; the pass checks it on every record and stops otherwise), so the distinct keys are counted chunk by chunk with the last bin carried over; no table is held.

Coverage and expressed transcripts are computed from all records of the BAM, called cells or not, as polyAfilter does (spec 3, from its code at commit 5ce0098: expressed transcripts are those with any same-strand coverage; `stream.coverage_prefix_chunked` reads every record). Only the bookkeeping is restricted to called cells.

**In-memory state per reference (one worker).** Arithmetic in [C] `layout_estimate.json` (script `layout_estimate.py`); nothing here is measured.
1. Handed over by fork, not copied: transcripts, the barcode-to-index and gene-to-index maps.
2. Phase 1, as in stage 13: two int32 coverage arrays and the sequence, "about 9 bytes per bp", freed before phase 2 [S13] (2.75 GiB worker peak on chr1 of 1179, [S13]); then the removal pieces per setting and strand.
3. Phase 2: fixed chunk buffers of 1,000,000 records, plus 17 bytes per record of a read group. Largest case, chr11 of 1179: 30,457,437 supporting reads ([C] `support_per_ref.json`) plus an estimated 0.30% of xf 19 records give 30.55 million records, 0.48 GiB. The read-group table of chr11 is an estimated 22.05 million rows (21,958,353 molecules plus 0.42% of xf 19 groups) times 51 bytes, 1.05 GiB. With the list of chunk arrays, the concatenated copy, the sort index (0.23 GiB), the sorted copy and the group table alive at once the sum is 3 x 0.48 + 0.23 + 1.05 = 2.7 GiB. For the nuclei libraries the same sum is 0.35 GiB.
4. Per-cell record counters: 4 RE classes x 2 x 24,378 cells x (14 settings + 3 totals) uint32: 4 x 2 x 24,378 x 17 x 4 bytes, 12.6 MiB.

Phase 1 and phase 2 do not overlap inside a worker, so a worker's peak stays near that of stage 13.

**Restart.** Same protocol as stage 13: each reference writes its parts and, last, `<ref>.json`; a reference whose json exists is skipped; the json names the inputs (settings list, sha256 of the barcode and feature files, BAM path, size and mtime), and `stream.stale_parts` stops the stage with the `gio trash` hint when parts were made from other inputs. Two more entries close a hole of version 1, where a rerun after a rule fix would have skipped finished references and mixed old parts with new ones (`stream.scan_to_parts` returns the stored stats when the json exists, `stream.py` lines 343 to 346): `rule_version`, a constant `RULE_VERSION` in `molstream.py` that every change of the read-group rule or of the row format bumps, and `module_sha256`, the sha256 of the `molstream.py` file itself. Any edit of the module therefore makes old parts stale; that is the intent.

**Note of 2026-10-13 (stage 23 as built).** The json carries a third entry of this kind, `unit_bin`, the `UNIT_BIN` of `pafbench/config.py`. The constant defines the units of `cell_records` and lives outside `molstream.py`, so `module_sha256` does not see a change of it and nothing forces a bump of `rule_version`. Without the entry a rerun after such a change kept the finished references and mixed units of two bin sizes (our observation on the synthetic library of the tests: chrA written at 1,000 stayed at 48 units where a fresh run at 100 gives 52; review of stage 23, finding F4).

**Inputs.** `possorted_genome_bam.bam`, `filtered_feature_bc_matrix/{barcodes,features}.tsv.gz`, `genome.fa`, `genes.gtf`, `IMPACT_SETTINGS`.

**Outputs** under `<lib>/recount/parts/`:
- `<ref>.read_groups.parquet`: key (uint64 as above: row of barcodes.tsv.gz, UMI code, row of features.tsv.gz), n_reads, n_winner_reads, n_pa (uint16), re_class (uint8), end3, end3_nonpa (int32), one uint16 column `rm_<tag>` per setting; 51 bytes per row, as in memory.
- `<ref>.cell_records.parquet`: cell, re, gx (bool), total, with_ub, units, units_rg (units over read-group records), one uint32 per setting (removed primary records); one extra row set with cell -1 for all barcodes.
- `<ref>.json`: records, primary, called-cell records, records in read groups, read groups, seconds, input names, rule_version, module_sha256.

**Tests.** On the synthetic 10x BAM: every designed read group has the designed n_reads, n_winner_reads, n_pa, end3, end3_nonpa and removed counts in both modes; the removed bit of every record equals `flags.flag_reference` for every test setting; the unit count equals a naive set count; restart after one finished reference gives identical merged output; parts made from other inputs are refused; parts written with another `RULE_VERSION` or another module hash are refused with the `gio trash` hint; a reference without genes or without called cells writes empty parts; a group at the uint16 limit stops the pass.

**Can and cannot show.** Delivers what the reads of every counted and every discarded read group look like before and after each setting. It decides nothing about UMIs; stage 24 does.

**Run time and memory (estimates).** Two passes per reference, as stage 13. Stage 13 with 396 settings and 8 workers took 421 s, 335 s and 1,381 s and peaked at 13.9, 13.1 and 17.9 GiB tree sum ([M2] section 1). The probe's one-pass figure is 1.7, 1.7 and 9.5 min [P]. ### 3.2 Stage 24: counting rule, matrices and totals

**Purpose.** Merge the parts, count UMIs by the documented rule before and after each setting, run the acceptance checks, and write the matrices and totals that every later stage reads.

**Method.**
1. *Merge.* Concatenate the read-group parts. Each gene sits on one reference, so a (cell, UMI, gene) key should occur once; if a key occurs on two references its read counts are summed, its end positions are set to missing, and the case is counted (none genome-wide in the check of 2.3).
2. *Join across references.* The competing gene of a cell and UMI can sit on another chromosome: of the xf 19 groups on chr21 and chr22 that have a counted partner, 490 of 545, 468 of 544 and 3,985 of 4,557 have it on another reference ([C] `m1_rule_check_<lib>.json`). The 8-byte keys of all parts are sorted to find every cell and UMI with two or more read groups (72,718 / 70,845 / 502,396 of them, 2.3). Only those rows go through the rule; a cell and UMI with a single group needs no join.
3. *Counting rule.* The documented one (wording in 2.3), applied to the corrected UMIs (UB) of the unfiltered run. The raw UMIs (UR) stay on every record of a filtered BAM as well (our observation: all of the first 500,000 records of `1417/original/c375_l3_m2_i.bam` and of the 1179 subset BAM of that setting carry UR, [V3] `ur_in_filtered_bam.json`), and a Cell Ranger rerun would start from them. Using UB is a choice, and check A6 measures what it costs. Before filtering a group has `n_reads` reads; at setting t it has `n_reads - rm_t`. Among the groups of one cell and UMI that still have a read, the gene with strictly the most reads gets the UMI; a tie for the maximum gives none. A single group gets the UMI while it has a read. One function gives both counts: before filtering from all reads, at a setting from the reads that survive.
4. *Events per setting.* With c0 the gene the rule counts before filtering and ct the gene it counts at setting t (either can be none):
   - lost: c0 exists and ct is not c0. The entry (cell, c0) falls by one.
   - gained: ct exists and ct is not c0. The entry (cell, ct) rises by one.
   - switched: both exist and differ. The UMI moves to another gene; it is one loss for c0 and one gain for ct, and is counted in both and reported on its own.
5. *Matrices.* `M0` is the rule's count on all reads, the unfiltered matrix of this analysis (cells by genes). It is Cell Ranger's filtered matrix except for the 5, 2 and 33 entries of 2.3 (check A1b). Per setting, `L_tag` holds the losses and `G_tag` the gains. The filtered matrix is `M0 - L_tag + G_tag`; a helper `matrices.load(lib, tag)` returns it. Storing losses and gains keeps files small and makes "what changed" the stored object. Because both ends come from one rule, a setting that removes no read of a cell and UMI cannot change its count. Version 2 took `M0` from Cell Ranger and the filtered state from the rule; on the cells and UMIs where the two disagree that gave a loss or a gain at every setting with nothing removed.

**Acceptance checks** (a failure of A1 to A5 stops the pipeline; `recount.json` gets its field `passed` only when all of them have run and passed, and stages 25 to 28 refuse to start without it):
- A1. The xf rule: read groups with `n_winner_reads` above 0 (xf bit 1 set, bit 2 unset) reproduce `filtered_feature_bc_matrix` in every (cell, gene) entry, all genes, all three libraries. Tolerance 0 (`XF_RECOUNT_TOLERANCE`). The check script already gives this genome-wide (2.3); here it shows that the pass reads cells, UMIs and genes as Cell Ranger does.
- A1b. Agreement of the documented rule with Cell Ranger on unfiltered reads: `M0` against `filtered_feature_bc_matrix`, entry by entry. Expected from 2.3: 5, 2 and 33 differing entries. Gate: at most 1 differing entry per million (`RULE_AGREEMENT_MAX`; measured 0.48, 0.20 and 0.43). Every differing cell and UMI is written to `recount.json` with its read groups, and the page states the count and the cause. A count other than 5, 2 and 33 that stays under the gate does not stop the stage; it is written to `recount.json` and to the page as a difference from the check script, with the cells and UMIs that are new. The check function takes the gate as an argument, and on the synthetic data the tests give it an absolute count. Version 2 set this check to tolerance 0 and would have stopped here.
- A2. The read groups with `n_winner_reads` above 0 equal the `molecule_info.h5` rows of pass_filter barcodes (join on cell, gene, 2-bit UMI: nothing left over on either side), and `n_reads` equals `count` for every one of them.
- A3. Tie to the validated engine: for every setting, removed primary records per called cell equal `grid/cell_counts.parquet` (and the totals equal its `total` column); over all barcodes, removed and total primary records by RE equal `grid/region_counts.parquet`. Exact.
- A4. Removal masks, reduction and rule, against the original tool's own files. A naive recount (plain Python dict, no shared code) of each original-tool BAM by the documented rule on its xf bit 1 records must equal `M0 - L_tag + G_tag`, entry by entry, tolerance 0. Both sides apply the same rule to the same surviving reads, so the entries of A1b do not enter. The 8 published settings on 1417 and on 1419 (`<lib>/original/<tag>.bam`), and the same 8 on the chr21 and chr22 subset of 1179 (`validation/subset_1179`): 24 BAMs, at most 4 recounts at once (`A4_PARALLEL`). For the 1179 subset the stored matrices are the wrong comparison, because the subset BAM holds no partner group from another reference and a group whose winning gene sits elsewhere is counted there. So the rule is applied again to the read groups of chr21 and chr22 alone, before and after removal, and that result is compared with the naive recount. The records of those BAMs carry the tags of the unfiltered run (2.1), so A4 tests the per-record masks, the reduction per group and the rule as coded. It does not test agreement with a Cell Ranger rerun; only stage 33 does.
- A5. Invariants. Gains can occur, so "filtered is at most unfiltered" does not hold per gene or per matrix entry. What must hold: `rm_t` is at most `n_reads` for every group; for the two controls (if D3 keeps them), `rm` at c250_l7_m1_e is at most `rm` at c250_lnone_m0_e and `rm` at c200_l10_m2_i at most `rm` at c200_lnone_m0_i, group by group (the control's pieces contain the setting's pieces by construction, `stream.reference_pieces`); a cell and UMI none of whose reads is removed keeps its count; the cells and UMIs counted after filtering are a subset of those with a read group before; every gain belongs to a cell and UMI with two or more read groups before filtering; a single-group cell and UMI is lost exactly when all its reads are removed; `L_tag` is at most `M0` entry by entry and `M0 - L_tag + G_tag` has no negative entry; per tag, the sum of `cell_totals.umis` equals the sum of `gene_totals.umis` and the matrix sum. A simulation with UMI correction and the low-support step ported from the source (284,746 reads, 140,270 cells and UMIs, 362 of them with three or more groups, two nested pairs of settings) keeps every one of these, and `M0 - L + G` equals a direct recount of the surviving reads; with c0 taken from the xf-like flag instead, 257 events appear where nothing was removed ([V3] `sim_v3_rule.json`). On the real read groups of 1417 chr21 and chr22, with a random fifth of the reads removed, the same holds at three seeds ([V3] `probe_ruling_1417.json`).
- A6. Reported, not a gate: what the choice of corrected UMIs costs after filtering. ### 3.3 Stage 25: cell classes from marker genes

**Purpose.** Give every called cell a coarse class, in all three libraries, from the unfiltered matrix. No annotations exist (user), so classes come from retinal marker genes.

**Method (D1, option 1).**
1. Input `M0`, all called cells. No cell is dropped: per-cell loss must cover every called cell. Genes detected in fewer than 3 cells (`MIN_CELLS_GENE`) are left out of the clustering only.
2. scanpy with every parameter written out in `config.CLUSTER`: `normalize_total` to 10,000, `log1p`, 2,000 highly variable genes, `scale`, PCA, neighbour graph on 30 components with k 15, Leiden at resolution 1.0 with seed 0, UMAP for display. The plan quotes the scanpy documentation of each function with its URL before any of them is used; the Leiden parameters are documented as "A parameter value controlling the coarseness of the clustering" (resolution) and "Change the initialization of the optimization" (random_state) (https://scanpy.readthedocs.io/en/stable/generated/scanpy.tl.leiden.html, [R] E). The seed is the Leiden seed. Whether PCA and the neighbour graph give the same result twice on the same matrix is tested in the first plan task, not assumed.
3. Class score per cell with `scanpy.tl.score_genes` for each class of the panel, on the panel genes present in the matrix. Cluster score = mean cell score. A cluster takes the class with the highest cluster score if that score is above 0 and exceeds the second class by a margin (config, proposed 0.1); otherwise "unassigned". Cells inherit the class of their cluster.
4. The scanpy model needed later is saved: variable genes, their mean and standard deviation, PCA loadings.

**Marker panel.** A proposal for the user to edit (D1). Classes are scored as the sources define them; every gene has its source wording in [R] C (adult human retina unless stated).

5. Donor replicate: class proportions and class-mean expression in 1417 against 1419.

### 3.4 Stage 26: impact of filtering

**Purpose.** Spec 8 and Q3: per-cell UMI and gene loss, intronic share, loss per class, cluster preservation, marker genes, and whether any class collapses. The paper's case: "after filtering, the 'Endothelial' cell type was no longer detected in this mouse cortex sample" (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9142200/, [R] B). For every setting of `IMPACT_SETTINGS` and all three libraries (spec 8).

**Method.** Spec 8 asks for per-class loss and for cluster preservation, so both views are built; this is design, not a question. The fixed-label view carries the headline numbers. Every threshold below is our proposal and is printed next to the result it shapes.

*Fixed labels.* Classes come from stage 25 on the unfiltered matrix and do not change.
1. Per cell: UMIs and genes before and after, UMIs lost and gained; intronic share before and after (molecules with `re_class` N or both, over all molecules); and the share of the cell's records removed, in two versions. The first is over records with RE E or N and a GX tag, the sense records. In 1179 RE N records carry no GX (2.3), so there this share covers exonic records only, and the page says so. It is close to the paper's statistic, not the same: the paper reports "removing about 64% of intragenic (exonic and intronic) alignments overall ..., ranging from 38% to 100% per cell" (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9142200/, [R] B), and its counting "only included reads aligning in the sense orientation" (Ding et al. 2020, [R] B). The second is over all records with RE E or N, which here include antisense records that polyAfilter never removes (20.9% of them on 1417 chr22, 2.3), so it reads lower for that reason alone.
2. Per class: cells; median and quartiles of UMIs and genes per cell before and after; pooled share of UMIs lost and gained; intronic share; cells that fall below the lowest UMI total of any called cell before filtering.
3. Marker genes: the panel genes of the class plus its top 20 genes (`TOP_GENES`) by a Wilcoxon rank on the unfiltered matrix. That list is a ranking picked on the data that defined the clusters; no p-value is reported for it. Per gene and setting: share of its UMIs lost within the class, detection rate before and after, log2 fold change class against rest before and after. Genes that lose more than half (`MARKER_LOSS`) of their UMIs in their own class are listed; this is the route by which a class could vanish.
4. Label retention by projection. Our own construction: filtered cells are normalised as in stage 25, restricted to the unfiltered variable genes, scaled with the unfiltered mean and sd, multiplied by the unfiltered PCA loadings, and classified by a k 15 nearest-neighbour vote among the unfiltered cells. Retention of a class = share of its cells that keep the class. Each cell's own unfiltered copy is among the reference cells, so retention is near 1 on an unchanged matrix and can only fall; and a shift common to all cells (per-cell totals lean on single genes: MALAT1 holds 6.7% / 6.8% / 4.7% of all UMIs, [V] `xf19_top_genes_<lib>.json`) lowers it without any class losing separability. So the number is never read alone:
   - the depth-matched thinned matrices (step 8) go through the same projection, and retention is printed for filtered next to thinned, class by class;
   - the class by class confusion table (fixed class against projected class) is printed for filtered and for thinned, so that a loss spread over all classes is told apart from one class moving into another.
5. Separability inside each matrix. The stage 25 pipeline is run on the matrix itself (its own variable genes, scaling and PCA) and a k 15 nearest-neighbour classifier predicts the fixed labels in 5-fold stratified cross-validation; recall per class and balanced accuracy, for the unfiltered matrix, each filtered matrix and each thinned matrix. No projection is involved, so a common shift cannot move it.

*Reclustering.*
6. Each filtered matrix goes through the stage 25 pipeline from scratch and is clustered at seeds 0 to 9, as the unfiltered matrix is. Version 1 clustered each filtered matrix once, so one unlucky split would have read as a collapse.
7. Agreement with the unfiltered clustering: ARI per seed. Per class, the Jaccard index between the fixed class and the best union of filtered clusters: clusters are sorted by the share of their cells that belong to the class, and the prefix with the highest Jaccard is taken (exact for this objective, since the best set is always such a prefix). No cluster is named for this, so losing marker UMIs cannot change it.
8. Two controls, because a clustering changes whenever its input changes: (a) seeds, the unfiltered matrix at seeds 0 to 9; (b) depth-matched thinning, the unfiltered matrix with each cell's UMIs drawn without replacement down to the cell's filtered total (a multivariate hypergeometric draw, so the total is exact; 1 replicate per setting and 3 for each of the two chosen settings, each clustered at seeds 0 to 9; a cell whose filtered total is not below its unfiltered one is left as it is). It shows what losing that many UMIs does when the loss is blind to the gene. Thinning is our design; no reference was found for it in [R].
9. **Collapse call (proposal).** Per class and setting, two separate outcomes:
   - *The cells no longer cluster together.* The best-union Jaccard is at most 0.5 in all 10 filtered seeds, while it stays above 0.75 in all 10 unfiltered seeds and in every thinned run of that setting.
   - *The cells still cluster together, but the marker rule no longer names them.* The best-union Jaccard stays above 0.75 in all 10 filtered seeds, and in all of them the marker rule of stage 25, applied to the filtered matrix, gives the clusters that hold most of the class another name or none, while it keeps the name in every thinned run. The report prints the marker loss behind it: per panel gene the share of UMIs lost within the class, and the class score and margin before and after.
   A class at or below 0.75 in an unfiltered seed run is reported as "not stable before filtering" and gets no call. A class that falls to 0.5 in the filtered runs and also leaves 0.75 in a thinned run is reported as "lost at this depth, with or without the filter's choice of reads". Everything in between is reported as "unclear" with its numbers.
   The cut-offs 0.5 and 0.75 are the defaults of `fpc::clusterboot` for a related statistic (CRAN fpc.pdf, https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/fpc/fpc.pdf, [R] E: "the Jaccard similarities of the original clusters to the most similar clusters in the resampled data are computed"; "dissolution ... smaller or equal to this value, the cluster is considered as 'dissolved'", default 0.5; recover default 0.75). clusterboot compares with the single most similar cluster; the best union is our change.
10. **Resolution.** Steps 6 to 9 are repeated at Leiden resolution 0.5 and 2.0 with the fixed classes unchanged, and the page prints for every call whether it holds at both ("cluster number changes with resolution, so a lost endothelial 'cluster' can be a resolution effect", [R] E, our reasoning there).

**Inputs.** `matrices/` (stage 24; the record totals come from `cell_totals.parquet`), `cells/` (stage 25).

**Outputs.**

### 3.5 Stage 27: truth-set strata by cell class and expression level

**Purpose.** Spec 7 strata that waited for stage 25 and for counted UMIs: sensitivity and specificity of the chosen and published settings by coarse cell class and by expression level.

**Method.** `evaluate.units` as in stage 17, same population and units (called cells, molecules with a member read within 1,000 nt, 8/4 labels), with two more molecule attributes: `cell_class` (class of the molecule's barcode) and `expr_bin`. ### 3.6 Stage 28: selection bias, labeled against unlabeled

**Purpose.** Spec 4.1 and 11. A molecule enters the truth set only if one of its reads was polyA-trimmed. Does that subset look like the rest? Two comparisons: among counted molecules, and at record level within called cells, which is the task milestone 2 handed over ("left for milestone 3, which builds per-cell counts", [M2] section 10).

**Method, counted molecules.** Population: molecules of called cells counted before filtering (`counted_molecules.parquet`). A counted molecule is "labeled" when a truth molecule joins it through a pa read (the join of 3.5, read from `eval/truth_to_counted.parquet`). Truth molecules that do not join are tabulated by region; they are intergenic, antisense, multi-gene, and in 1179 intronic ones, none of which Cell Ranger counts (2.3). The pass's own `n_pa` above 0 is a second definition of labeled that needs no join; the two are cross-tabulated.

Five axes, each a table of category shares among labeled and among unlabeled molecules:
1. Region: supported by exonic records only, intronic only, or both.
2. Cell class.
3. Gene: expression bin of the gene; and per gene the labeled fraction, with the 20 genes furthest above and below the library's overall fraction.
4. Read position relative to the annotated 3' end. This axis compares where the ordinary reads of the two groups end; it does not compare priming sites, because an unlabeled molecule has no observed site. The position is `end3_nonpa`, the 3'-most base over the reads without a pa tag, for both groups. Version 1 used the 3'-most base of any read, which for a labeled molecule is the priming site and for an unlabeled one a read end upstream of it; the nearest ordinary read ends a median of 121 to 157 nt upstream of the site (2.3), so the two groups would have differed in the first two bins by construction. Distance: from that base to the nearest annotated transcript end of the gene at or downstream, along the gene's collapsed exons when the base is exonic and along the genome otherwise (the member-distance convention of spec 4.1), in bins 0 to 100, 101 to 300, 301 to 1,000, 1,001 to 5,000, above 5,000, and past every annotated end. Labeled molecules whose only read is the pa read have no such base and form their own category, shown with its share and left out of the bins. The 3'-most of two reads lies closer to the end than a single read, so the comparison is made at equal numbers of non-pa reads (1, 2, 3 or more); the rule below reads the stratum with one.
5. UMI family size (`n_reads`: 1, 2, 3 or more), since the spec expects bigger families to be favoured.

**Method, records within called cells.** One table per library: by RE (E, N, I), the share of labeled molecules of called cells (RE of the pa read, `molecules_labeled.parquet`) against the share of called-cell primary records, and against the share of duplicate-free units (3.1). Milestone 2 made this comparison over all barcodes and removed duplicates with the SAM flag. Stated plainly: that flag is absent on every RE I record in all three libraries and on every RE N record of 1179 (7,716,349, 6,936,436 and 35,808,002 RE I records and 135,259,475 RE N records of 1179, the same number with and without the flag, [M2] section 10), and among supporting records it coincides with xf 17 (201,421 / 186,663 / 2,337,283 flagged supporters, the number of xf 17 supporters, [P]; record by record in [C] `dupflag_vs_xf_<lib>.json`; the source sets the flag only on reads that went through duplicate marking and are neither the UMI count nor low support, [CR] `tx_annotation/src/read.rs` lines 560 to 568). The milestone 2 shares "without PCR duplicates" therefore remove duplicates in counted regions only, which inflates the share of every uncounted region. The three differences above 5 points that milestone 2 reported (1179 E 14.8 points, 1179 N 12.7, 1419 E 5.2) are inflated by that: against all records, with no duplicate removal, 1179 E and N stay above 5 points (8.1 and 7.4) and 1419 E does not (2.7) ([M2] section 10). They are redone here with the unit of 3.1, which is defined the same way in every region. Its limits are listed there, and units per read group are printed next to the table.

**What the 5 point rule decides.** The threshold of 5 percentage points is the one milestone 2 used ([M2] section 10, "our choice"; spec 11 gives no number). An axis fires for a library when any of its categories differs by more than 5 points between labeled and unlabeled molecules; the record table fires when an RE share of labeled molecules differs by more than 5 points from the unit share. A fired axis decides three things and nothing else:
1. Wording: every headline sentence on burden (Q1) and accuracy (Q2) for that library says "among molecules with a polyA-trimmed read" and names the axis.
2. Burden numbers side by side in the representativeness section. The headline and the unweighted share among counted labeled molecules are always printed. The reweighted share is printed when an axis among 1, 2, 3 and 5 fires; if only axis 4 or the record table fires, the wording of point 1 changes and no reweighted number is given. The last two are named "among counted molecules" and never enter a headline sentence:
   - the headline: templated share among all labeled molecules of called cells;
   - the unweighted share among counted labeled molecules: the same share on the labeled molecules that join a counted molecule;
   - the reweighted share among counted molecules: that subset reweighted to the mix of all counted molecules over the fired axes.
   The step from the first to the second is a change of population, not a bias: it drops the labeled molecules Cell Ranger never counts (21.8% / 21.9% / 27.5% of them, 2.3), and in 1179 those hold the highest templated shares (0.917 in introns against 0.292 in 3' UTRs, [M2] section 10). Only the step from the second to the third is the selection effect. Axis 4 never enters the reweighting: it is a comparison of read positions, and the "pa read only" category has no counterpart among unlabeled molecules. Assumption, stated in the report: within a stratum, labeled molecules stand for unlabeled ones, that is, their templated share is the same. Strata are the cross of the fired axes among 1, 2, 3 and 5; weight of a stratum = its share among all counted molecules over its share among counted labeled molecules. Pooling: a stratum with fewer than 30 labeled molecules is merged with the other small strata that share its category on the first fired axis (order: region, cell class, expression bin, family size); if the merged stratum is still under 30 it joins the largest stratum of that category. The number of merged strata and the share of counted molecules in them are printed. Interval: gene bootstrap with the weights recomputed in every resample.
3. Placement: the accuracy table for that axis (region from milestone 2, cell class and expression from stage 27) moves from the details block into the main text.

It never changes labels, the chosen settings or the selection rule. If no axis fires, the report says by how many points the two groups differ at most on the axes checked, which is not proof of no bias on other axes.

**Inputs.** `recount/counted_molecules.parquet`, `matrices/cell_totals.parquet` (record and unit totals), `eval/truth_to_counted.parquet` (stage 27), `truth/molecules_labeled.parquet`, `cells/cells.parquet`, `matrices/gene_totals.parquet`, `genes.gtf`.

**Outputs.** `burden/selection_bias_m3.parquet` (lib, axis, stratum, category, n_labeled, n_unlabeled, share_labeled, share_unlabeled, diff_points); `burden/selection_bias_records.parquet` (lib, re, labeled, records, units, share_labeled, share_records, share_units, diff_points_records, diff_points_units); `burden/selection_bias_genes.parquet` (lib, gene_id, gene_name, umis, labeled, labeled_fraction); `burden/templated_share_reweighted.parquet` (lib, axes, share_headline, share_counted_unweighted, share_counted_reweighted, lo, hi, strata, strata_pooled, pooled_share); `burden/selection_bias_m3.json` (join table by region, rule outcome per library and axis, threshold).

**Acceptance.** Labeled plus unlabeled equals the sum of `M0`; joined plus not joined truth molecules equals the labeled molecules in called cells (90,099 / 105,400 / 1,174,777, [M2] section 4); summed over cells, the record totals by RE equal the called-cell totals of stage 13 (`grid/cell_counts.parquet`, column total).

**Tests.** A planted difference of 5.1 points fires and 4.9 does not; the join takes GX of the pa read; distances on a synthetic gene with two annotated ends; a labeled molecule with only a pa read lands in its own category; axis 4 is refused as a reweighting axis; pooling on a toy table; the reweighted share equals the unweighted one when the mixes are equal.

**Can and cannot show.** Shows on which measured axes the labeled molecules differ from the other counted molecules, and whether the labeled set over-represents a region at record level once duplicates are removed everywhere. It cannot compare the property that matters most, the priming site, because unlabeled molecules have no observed site.

**Run time and memory.** Minutes; memory set by the 1179 table of counted molecules (7.8 GiB as arrays with every column; a join copies the key columns and the result, so two to three times that, 16 to 23 GiB, is our estimate until the first run under memwatch).

### 3.7 Stage 29: burden corrected for label purity, by region

**Purpose.** Milestone 2 corrected the templated share for label purity per library only (0.896 to 0.871 in 1417, 0.887 to 0.859 in 1419, 0.497 to 0.409 in 1179, [M2] section 10). This stage does it per region, with intervals.

**Method.** Per region (intergenic, intron, ncexon, 5UTR, CDS, 3UTR, antisense), per library and per group (nuclei, cells), on labeled molecules of called cells. Per A band b in a region, the tail-primed share is (h_b - h_ref) / (h_tail - h_ref) clipped to 0..1, with h the AATAAA/ATTAAA rate, h_ref the rate at 14 or more A's and h_tail the rate at 0 to 4 A's. Corrected templated share of the region = sum over the templated bands of n_b x (1 - tail share of b), divided by the region's labeled molecules. `evaluate.label_purity` takes its reference rates from the frame it is given, so applied to one region it yields the region-reference version only; the new function `evaluate.tail_share(mol, ref_rate, tail_rate)` around `_tail_estimate` takes the rates as arguments and serves both versions.

Two versions are computed and both are printed in any case: reference rates from the whole library, and reference rates from the region itself. Which one is the headline for a region is fixed here, before any number exists: the region's own rates where the region has at least 200 molecules in each reference class and the library-wide rate lies outside the gene-bootstrap 95% interval of the region's rate for either class; the library-wide rates elsewhere. Version 1 made the library-wide version the headline everywhere, on an assumption it called unverified. The table of h_tail and h_ref per region, with intervals, is the first output and shows which case each region is in. Intervals: gene bootstrap, 1,000 resamples (`evaluate.gene_clusters`, `resample_counts`), with the reference rates recomputed in every resample.

**Inputs.** `truth/molecules_labeled.parquet` (in_cell, a20, hexamer, region, gene, ref, strand, site).

**Outputs.** `burden/purity_reference_by_region.parquet` (group, region, n_ref_tail, n_ref_internal, h_tail, h_tail_lo, h_tail_hi, h_ref, h_ref_lo, h_ref_hi, headline_version); `burden/templated_share_corrected.parquet` (group, region, n_labeled, share_label, share_corrected, lo, hi, share_corrected_regionref, lo_regionref, hi_regionref, headline_version); `burden/purity_by_region.parquet` (group, region, a_band, n, hexamer_rate, tail_primed_share).

**Acceptance.** For each library, the all-regions value computed with the class-level formula of `evaluate.label_purity` equals `templated_share_corrected` of `eval/selection.json` to 9 decimals, and the per-band sum equals it whenever no band estimate is clipped (it reproduces 0.871 for 1417 from the band values of [M2] section 2; recomputed by the reviewer, [V] `notes.md` section 4).

**Tests.** Per-band sum equals the class-level formula when nothing is clipped; `tail_share` with the frame's own rates equals `label_purity`; a region without reference molecules gets no region-specific value; the headline version follows the stated rule on a toy table; bootstrap is seeded.

**Can and cannot show.** An estimate of how much of the templated class in each region is tail priming next to genome-encoded A's. Where the library-wide rates are used it rests on the assumption that the hexamer rate of true tail-primed and of true internally primed molecules does not depend on region; the reference table shows for which regions that could be tested and for which it could not.

**Run time and memory.** Under stage 18's 124 s and 1.9 GiB ([M2] section 1) plus the bootstrap; minutes.

### 3.8 Stage 30: whole-isoform context of long-read 3' ends

**Purpose.** Spec 4.3: for BD67 and BD70 3' ends, does the end sit in a 3' UTR, CDS or intron of an otherwise complete isoform?

**What is on disk** (our observations today; counts in [C] `bd70_file_counts.json`, script `bd70_file_counts.py`, which runs `wc -l` and one pass over each table):
- BD67: only the FLNC BAM and its index. No isoform-level file.
- BD70, `/data/long_read_annotation_tool/test_dataset/input_files`: collapsed GFFs (`...BD70.sorted.gff`, 2,066,358 lines; `...trimmed.min20count.gtf`, 1,134,586 lines; `...trimmed.right_bounds.gff`, 15,060,667 lines; `...min10counts.gtf`), two classification tables (`classification/BD70.trimmed.min20count_classification.txt`, 186,079 isoforms; `BD70_classification.filtered_lite_classification.txt`, 1,167,353 isoforms), isoform by cell matrices, and `BD70_strict_barcode.tsv` (read name to barcode and UMI).
- No read-to-isoform table was found.
- The classification tables have the columns structural_category, subcategory, associated_gene, associated_transcript, diff_to_TTS, diff_to_gene_TTS, perc_A_downstream_TTS, seq_A_downstream_TTS and CDS columns, but `coding` reads "non_coding" for all 186,079 isoforms of the min20 table and CDS_genomic_start is NA throughout: these files do not say where the CDS is.
- The filtered_lite table was made with an intra-priming filter (header of `..._reasons.txt`: "# intrapriming cutoff: 0.6"; 60,590 isoforms removed as IntraPriming), so it is not a neutral catalogue of 3' ends.
- The filename guide says the "trimmed" files come from `BD70.fltnc.aligned.trimmed.bam`, "reads aligned to the reference (polyA tails clipped)". That BAM is not in the folder and the clipping rule is not recorded there.
- Structural categories of the min20 table: incomplete-splice_match 66,940, full-splice_match 40,501, novel_in_catalog 23,851, novel_not_in_catalog 23,361, intergenic 15,600, genic 11,947, antisense 3,010, fusion 810, genic_intron 50, moreJunctions 9. Definitions from https://isoseq.how/classification/categories.html (not in [R]; read on the page itself on 2026-10-12, copy in `M3D/review/v2/dl_verify/isoseq_categories.html`): full-splice match, "The reference and query isoform have the same number of exons and each internal junction matches the positions of the reference."; incomplete-splice match, "The query isoform has fewer external exons than the reference, but each internal junction matches the positions of the reference."; novel in catalog, "The query isoform does not have a FSM or ISM match, but is using a combination of known donor/acceptor sites."; novel not in catalog, "The query isoform does not have a FSM or ISM match, and has at least one donor or acceptor site that is not annotated." The page gives no text definition of the subcategories, so they are tabulated and not interpreted.

**Method (D5: our per-read rule; tecap is the second view if the user takes option 1).** Classify each accepted read by its own alignment against the 2024-A GTF (GENCODE v44, filtered: `reference.json` of the 10x reference names `gencode.v44.primary_assembly.annotation.gtf.filtered`), with one rule for both libraries.
1. Reads, sites and acceptance exactly as stage 16 (`bd67.terminal_a_run`, `accept_long_read`): the site is the start of the terminal A run. The read body is the alignment up to the site; aligned A's after the site belong to the run. Introns of the read are its CIGAR N operations and deletions are part of a block (our convention, as in the reviewer's `check_longread_sites.py`). **Note of 2026-10-15 (stage 30 as built).** When the run starts at the first base of an aligned block that follows a CIGAR N, that N ends at the site and is the last intron of the read: the body ends with an empty block at the site (`lrcontext.read_body`), so the end is the base before the site, as for every read, and the class is read where stage 16 read the region. Without this rule such a read was classed at the donor of that N, 41 nt to 198 kb upstream of its site, and genome-wide two BD70 sites of the region intron took an antisense or intergenic top class, which stopped the stage at its acceptance check (review of stage 30, finding F1). Such reads are 679 of BD67 at 200 sites and 893 of BD70 at 151 sites (our observation); with the rule 640 and 858 of them are no_model for a novel junction (neither splice site annotated for 624 and 847), the others antisense or intergenic.
2. Compatible transcripts, on the read's strand:
   - A read with introns is compatible with a transcript when its introns equal a run of consecutive introns of that transcript, coordinates exact. No annotated intron can then be skipped or read through between the read's first and last junction.
   - A read without introns is compatible with a transcript whose span contains the read (the 3' side with the 10 nt end tolerance). Version 1 asked for an exon containing the read's last 50 aligned nt, which gave a wholly intronic read, or an unspliced read running from an exon into an intron, no transcript at all.
   - A read without introns that no span contains is placed by its end: if a span contains the end, that transcript is taken and the read is flagged "starts outside"; if the read overlaps a span and ends past it, its class is downstream.
   - A spliced read with no fully compatible transcript is placed by its 3' part (new in version 3; measured below). Take the longest suffix of its intron chain, the introns nearest the 3' end and at least the last one, that equals a run of consecutive introns of a same-strand transcript, coordinates exact. The transcripts that hold that longest suffix are its compatible transcripts, and the read is flagged chain_novel with the suffix length. A spliced read whose last intron is in no same-strand transcript has no compatible transcript. The placement is tried only for a spliced read that overlaps a same-strand transcript span; one that overlaps none is antisense or intergenic.
3. Choice among compatible transcripts: one whose 3' end lies within 10 nt of the read end first, then the largest exonic overlap with the read, then the nearest annotated 3' end, then the transcript id. Version 1 began with "most matched introns", which is the same for every compatible transcript of a spliced read; and without the first criterion a read ending a few nt past one transcript's end would be assigned to a longer transcript for the sake of those few nt. The same choice serves the reads placed by their 3' part; for them the exonic overlap counts the whole read body, the unmatched upstream part included.
4. Context of the end, relative to that transcript, one class per read:

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| annotated_end | within 10 nt of the transcript's 3' end. Where the transcript carries the GENCODE tag `mRNA_end_NF` ("the mRNA end could not be confirmed", https://www.gencodegenes.org/pages/tags.html; 161 of 2,911 transcripts on chr21) the class stays and the read is flagged end_NF, because that end is not a known 3' end |
| 3UTR | in its 3' UTR, more than 10 nt from the end |
| CDS | in its coding sequence |
| 5UTR | in its 5' UTR |
| ncexon | in an exon of a transcript without CDS, not at its end |
| intron_unspliced | the read has no intron, its end is not in an exon of the transcript, and it lies inside the transcript's span |
| intron_after_junction | a spliced read that runs past a donor: its end lies in an intron of the transcript downstream of its last matched junction |
| downstream | past the transcript's annotated end by more than 10 nt |
| no_model | no compatible transcript, for one of three reasons, tested in this order and kept apart. Intergenic or antisense: the read overlaps no same-strand transcript span (antisense if it overlaps an opposite-strand one). Novel junction: a spliced read that overlaps a same-strand span and whose 3'-most intron no same-strand transcript has. Sub-cause of a novel junction, the first that applies: the intron lies within 5 nt of an annotated one at both ends (`NEAR_INTRON_TOL`); it is shorter than 50 nt (`MIN_INTRON`); it is an annotated intron of the opposite strand; both splice sites are annotated but never joined in one intron; the donor only is annotated; the acceptor only; neither |

5. "Otherwise complete": per read, the number of matched introns, whether its 5' end lies within 50 nt of the transcript's annotated 5' end, and whether it covers the start codon. Four flags travel with the class and change none: chain_novel with the suffix length (step 2); starts_outside (step 2); retained_after_junction, set when the block after the read's last junction runs over an annotated intron of the chosen transcript, which the class alone would hide (0.45% to 0.59% of fully compatible spliced reads in the measurements below); and end_NF (class table: 70 of the 279,677 annotated_end reads of BD70 chr21, and 2 of 13,149 sites would change their top class if those reads took the class of the exon instead, [V3] `endnf_effect_BD70_chr21.json`). chain_novel reads are never counted as 5' complete: their upstream part follows no annotated transcript (59.6% of them on BD70 chr21 hold an unannotated intron upstream).
6. Per unique site: reads per class, the top class, share of reads with at least one matched intron, share 5' complete, median read length. The top class is the one with the most reads among the eight classes with a model and the three no_model reasons, which compete separately; a tie goes to the one that comes first in the table above (among the reasons: novel junction, antisense, intergenic), so a no_model reason never wins a tie against a class with a model. The main sub-cause of a site with a novel-junction top class is the sub-cause with the most reads, ties in the order of the table. Tied sites and sites whose top class holds at most half of the reads are counted and printed (140 and 193 of 13,149 sites on BD70 chr21). Every site table is given in two versions (column `version`): "placed", with the chain_novel reads under their class, and "strict", with them counted as no_model for a novel junction as version 2 did; and the number of sites whose top class rests on chain_novel reads alone is printed (1,000 of 13,149 on BD70 chr21). Reads matched on their last intron only are the weak case and are shown apart. Then the table of label (templated, ambiguous, non-templated) by context, per library and A band. The reading the user asked for comes from two groups of entries: templated sites in CDS or 3UTR whose reads are spliced and 5' complete (a normal mRNA primed internally), and templated sites in intron_unspliced or intron_after_junction (unspliced or intron-retaining transcripts).
7. Region against context. The context can differ from the milestone 2 region at the same site: the region follows a fixed priority over all transcripts of the strand ("highest code wins", `pafbench/regions.py`), the context follows the transcript the read itself is compatible with. The cross-table of milestone 2 region by context is reported per library, with every site whose top class is a no_model reason listed with its region, reason and sub-cause.
8. BD70 cross-check: each isoform end of the min20 collapsed GTF is matched to the nearest long-read site within 10 nt on the same strand; the table of pigeon structural_category and subcategory by our top class is reported, together with the share of isoform ends that find a site.
9. Link to 1179, with a null. Truth molecules whose site lies within 10 nt of a long-read site (the stage 16 match, [M2] section 9) inherit the context of that site. Milestone 2 warned that agreement within 10 nt "may mostly measure co-location (not tested here)" ([M2] section 9), so the match is repeated with every 1179 site moved 1,000 nt upstream in transcript sense on the same reference and strand, as the PolyASite check did ([M2] section 10); the matched share and the label agreement of the shifted sites are printed next to the real ones, by label.
10. tecap, if D5 keeps it (option 1). `tecap classify` writes totals: `<sample>_terminal_exon.json` (reads per bucket, fractions, the PAS split, 3' UTR length bins), `<sample>_tecap_mqc.json`, two PNG figures and, with `--per-gene-table`, `<sample>_per_gene.tsv`. It writes no table per read, but it breaks the buckets down by the value of any BAM tag: `--cell-barcode-tag CELL_BARCODE_TAG  Per-cell breakdown via this BAM tag (e.g. CB)` ([C] `tecap_help.txt`), written as `per_cell_metrics` in the JSON. So the stage writes one BAM of accepted reads per library with the site key (reference, strand, site) in a tag of our own, indexes it, and runs tecap once per library: `tecap classify --bam <BD>/tecap/accepted.bam --sample <BD> --platform cdna-pacbio --gtf genes.gtf --polya-sites /data/scNoiseMeter/atlas.clusters.3.0.GRCh38.GENCODE_42.bed.gz --cell-barcode-tag <tag> --per-gene-table --threads <n> --out-dir <BD>/tecap/out`, with the 2024-A `genes.gtf`, which tecap accepted in the trials, and the PolyASite atlas of spec section 2. The label is a property of the site, so buckets per site give buckets per label by summing, and buckets per site can be set against our top class per site. Outputs of ours: `<BD>/tecap_site_buckets.parquet` (ref, strand, site, bucket, reads) and `<BD>/tecap_buckets.parquet` (label, bucket, reads). Our observations on the 624,785 accepted reads of BD70 chr21 ([V3] `lens_stage30_whole/`): with the label in the tag, one run gives 253,949 templated, 189,289 ambiguous and 139,650 non-templated classified reads, 582,888 in all; with the site key in the tag, 9,152 site keys, all among the 13,149 sites of stage 16, and summed by label they equal the first run. ### 3.9 Stage 31: matched sites and genes, nuclei against cells

**Purpose.** Spec 8: site sequence, hexamer and distance to the annotated end at sites and genes shared between libraries. Milestone 2 compared labels at matched sites (99.3% the same) and templated shares per gene ([M2] section 10).

**Method.** At one genomic position the sequence, the hexamer and the distance are the same in every library, so the comparison is about which sites each material uses.
1. Site table per group (nuclei = 1417 + 1419, cells = 1179; and 1417 against 1419 as the same-material baseline), from labeled molecules of called cells: molecules per site, label, a20, down20, up40, hexamer, region, gene, and the distance to the nearest annotated 3' end of the gene (signed; along collapsed exons when the site is exonic, genome otherwise).
2. Shared genes: at least 20 labeled molecules in both groups (the `same_gene` threshold; 1,649 genes for nuclei against cells, [M2] section 10).
3. Within shared genes, sites are matched within 5 nt on the same strand and fall into three sets: shared, first group only, second group only. `burden.same_site` matches each site of the first group to the nearest of the second and returns label-pair counts; this stage needs the pairs and a match that reads the same from both sides, so it uses the new `burden.match_sites` (mutual nearest within the tolerance, pairs returned). The 5 nt tolerance also covers the 1 to 2 nt shift that the unchecked bead oligo design could cause (spec 11).
4. Compared across the three sets: label and A band, A frequency at each of the 20 positions after the site, hexamer rate, region, distance bin.
5. At shared sites: the site's share of its gene's labeled molecules in nuclei against cells (Spearman, and the log2 ratio by label and region).
6. Depth: 1179 has 11.1 and 13.0 times the labeled molecules of 1419 and 1417 in called cells (1,174,777 against 105,400 and 90,099, [M2] section 4), so "cells only" sites may be a depth effect; this step measures it. The share of sites found in both is recomputed with the cell molecules of each gene downsampled to the nuclei count (seeded, 100 draws, `DOWNSAMPLE_DRAWS`).

**Note of 2026-10-15 (stage 31 as built).** Step 6 also redoes the usage comparison of step 5 in every draw, with the cell usage taken over the drawn molecules, and `matched_sites.json` reports its mean and 95% range over the draws (Spearman rho, and the median log2 ratio over all shared sites, by label and by region). A gene with more molecules shows more sites, each with a smaller share, so the full-depth values mostly measure depth: nuclei against cells give rho 0.661 and a median log2 ratio of 0.670 at full depth, and rho 0.849 (0.846 to 0.852 over 100 draws) with a median of 0 in every draw at the nuclei depth, against 0.885 and 0.163 for 1417 against 1419 at its own depth (review of stage 31, finding S31-1).

**Inputs.** `truth/molecules_labeled.parquet` of the three libraries, `genes.gtf`.

**Outputs.** `burden/matched_sites.parquet` (pair, gene, ref, strand, site_a, site_b, set, n_a, n_b, usage_a, usage_b, label, a20, hexamer, region, dist_end, dist_mode); `burden/matched_sites_summary.parquet` (pair, set, feature, bin, sites, molecules); `burden/matched_sites.json` (shared genes, Spearman, downsampling result).

**Acceptance.** Without the gene filter and with the one-way match of `burden.same_site`, the matched counts reproduce `burden/same_site.parquet` (nuclei against cells: 164,728 sites, 41,307 matched; 1417 against 1419: 79,509 and 11,301, [M2] section 10).

**Tests.** Three synthetic sites give one of each set; `match_sites(a, b)` and `match_sites(b, a)` give the same pairs; usage shares sum to 1 per gene; downsampling is seeded.

**Can and cannot show.** Shows which kinds of sites are used in both materials and which mainly in one, with donor against donor as the yardstick. It cannot say why: tissue, maturity, cell mix and nucleus against cell are confounded.

**Run time and memory.** Minutes, a few GiB (three molecule tables, 66 MB on disk together, `ls -l`).

### 3.10 Stage 32: ERCC mix identity

**What is known.** Lexogen states that SIRV-Set 4 holds the long SIRVs, Iso Mix E0 and the ERCCs, and "The ERCC Module includes ERCC Mix 1 (Munro et al., 2014)" (https://www.lexogen.com/sirvs/sirv-sets/, [R] F, read). Our table is the Mix 1 column of the Thermo Fisher file `cms_095046.txt`, and its 92 IDs equal the 92 ERCC IDs of the Lexogen FASTA ([R] F, our observation there). Not verified: that this Thermo Fisher file is the table Lexogen's module follows, and why the vendor's largest amount is 15.0 fmol ("ranging from 0.014 amol to 15.0 fmol", https://faqs.lexogen.com/faq/what-are-the-concentrations-of-the-sirvs) while the table's largest value is 30,000 amol per ul. The units differ (per tube against per ul of stock), so this may be a dilution only.

**What would settle it.**
1. In our data, which mix: the Thermo Fisher file lists both mixes and four subgroups of 23 ERCCs with Mix 1 to Mix 2 ratios of 4, 1, 0.67 and 0.5 (log2 2, 0, -0.58, -1; read from `OUT/ref/ercc_conc_raw.txt`). Regress log2 of the unfiltered ERCC UMIs on log2 of the Mix 1 concentration and take the residuals. If the tube holds Mix 1, the residuals do not depend on the subgroup; if it held Mix 2, they would follow minus the table's log2 ratio. Estimate the slope of the residual on that log2 ratio with a bootstrap interval over ERCCs: about 0 under Mix 1, about -1 under Mix 2. The mix is confirmed when the interval contains 0 and excludes -1 in both 1417 and 1419. A check that subgroup is unrelated to ERCC length guards against a capture-length artefact.
2. The table and the factor of 2: only the vendor can settle them. One short question to Lexogen: the per-ERCC amounts of SIRV-Set 4, and what "15.0 fmol" refers to. It is written as a draft for the user to send or not (D7); nothing is sent from here. The correlation of stage 15 uses relative amounts, so a common factor does not move it.

**Inputs.** `<lib>/spikein/ercc_counts.parquet` (setting "unfiltered"), `OUT/ref/ercc_conc_raw.txt`, the SIRV FASTA (lengths).

**Outputs.** `<lib>/spikein/ercc_mix.json`: n, Pearson r of log UMIs against Mix 1 and against Mix 2, slope with interval, mean residual per subgroup, verdict. The report drops the word "provisional" from the ERCC section only when both verdicts are "Mix 1"; stage 15 files are not rewritten.

**Acceptance.** n equals `ercc_with_concentration` of `ercc.json` (68 in 1417, 62 in 1419, [M2] section 8).

**Tests.** Counts simulated from Mix 1 and from Mix 2 get the right verdict; with too few ERCCs the verdict is "undecided".

**Can and cannot show.** Settles Mix 1 against Mix 2 from the data. Does not settle lot-specific deviations or absolute amounts.

**Run time.** Seconds.

**Note of 2026-10-15 (stage 32 as built).** Step 1 expects a residual slope of about -1 under Mix 2, which holds when UMIs grow in proportion to input. The fitted log2 slope of UMIs on Mix 1 is 0.902 in 1417 and 0.917 in 1419, and under Mix 2 the residuals then fall by about that slope times the log2 ratio. With -1 as the only Mix 2 value, counts simulated from Mix 2 with the fitted parameters of the two libraries got "undecided" in 39 and 31 of 100 libraries (review of stage 32, finding S32-1). So `pafbench.ercc.ercc_mix` names Mix 2 when the interval excludes 0 and reaches the range from -1 to minus the fitted slope (`log2_slope_mix1` in `ercc_mix.json`); in the same simulations Mix 2 is then named in 95 and 97 of 100, and no library is given the other mix. The confirmation of Mix 1 is unchanged: the interval contains 0 and excludes -1.

## 4. Report page

**Form.** One HTML file, published as an Artifact (private until shared); plotly.js loads from cdnjs, everything else is in the file. Each section opens with a few plain sentences: what was done, why, how to read the figure. Then the figure, then the sentences that state the result, then a details block with the full tables. Written for a single-cell RNA-seq researcher who has not read the spec.

**Generated by code, every number computed at render time.** Lesson of milestone 2: a hand-written sentence with a wrong ranking reached a draft.
1. Prose lives in templates with named fields only. Every value goes through one function, `num(value, source, fmt)`, which formats it and records in `numbers.json` the value, the file, column and filter it was read from, and the stage script and function that computed it (spec 1: every number traces to the function that computes it).
2. Words that depend on data (higher, lower, largest, the order of a list) come from helper functions that compare at render time. Templates contain no such word outside a helper call.
3. A test renders the page from a synthetic output tree and fails if the prose of a milestone 3 template holds a digit that is neither in the registry nor on a short allow-list of constants (setting names, 8/4, 1,000 nt, library names). The milestone 2 blocks of rule 5 are wrapped and exempt from this test by name (the eleven functions of `m2report.SECTIONS` and `m2report.headline`): their prose carries digits computed inside those functions, which `tests/test_m2report.py` covers. A second render with changed inputs must change the sentences accordingly.
4. Each figure block lists the files and columns it read. A missing input prints "not run (missing ...)" as in `m2report.py`.
5. Milestone 2 tables are not retyped: the page calls the section functions of `pafbench/m2report.py` and converts their Markdown to HTML with a small converter limited to what those functions emit.
6. Figures are Plotly figures built in Python and embedded as JSON. Under each figure a table holds the plotted numbers, so the page reads without scripts. Plots show aggregates (quantiles, histograms), never one point per molecule.

**Sections and figures.** Files are under `OUT` unless a full path is given.

### D5. Isoform context of long-read ends
Decided 2026-10-12: option 1.
Question: How do we get whole-isoform context for BD67 and BD70 3' ends?
Context: `tecap classify` takes an aligned long-read BAM (`--bam`, platform cdna-pacbio or cdna-ont), a GENCODE GTF and a PolyASite atlas BED, and puts each read into one bucket by where its 3' end lies relative to the terminal exon ([C] `tecap_help.txt`: `tecap --help`, `tecap classify --help`, `tecap explain`). A trial on the chr21 records of BD70 (`M3D/tecap_trial/`) classified 670,045 reads in 5 min 50 s: 44.6% Captured, 24.2% MechB-exon, 13.2% MechA-correct, 11.8% MechB-APA, 4.2% MechB-aspecific, 1.7% internal priming in the terminal-exon CDS, 0.3% in the two other MechA buckets, 21 reads MechC. It writes bucket totals, a per-gene table and two figures, and with `--cell-barcode-tag` the buckets per value of any BAM tag. With our site key in a tag, one run gives buckets per site, and so per label (tested on BD70 chr21, 3.8). It classifies only spliced reads that overlap a gene, so unspliced reads that end in introns are not in its view (9.0% of the reads at templated sites on BD70 chr21 have no splice).
Options:
1. (recommended) Both: our per-read rule against GENCODE as the stage 30 table (each accepted read classed by its own splice chain and site, one rule for BD67 and BD70, the BD70 pigeon classification as a cross-check), and tecap as a second view in your bucket names, tied to our sites and labels through a tag on the accepted reads (`--cell-barcode-tag`). tecap itself is not changed.
2. Our per-read rule only.
3. tecap only: buckets per site and label as in option 1; no per-read rule of ours.
Recommendation: option 1.
Why: The spec asks whether the end sits in an otherwise complete isoform, which needs each read's splice chain and its own site; the per-read rule gives that and treats both libraries alike. tecap adds your terminal-exon buckets per site and label with no change to the tool, for spliced reads in genes. It is shown next to the labels and not used for them, because its buckets use PolyASite proximity, which the spec keeps out of label validation.

## 6. Risks and what would change the plan

**Assumptions nobody has verified yet.**
1. The pass of stage 23 reproduces what the check scripts measured genome-wide: the xf recount exact, 5, 2 and 33 entries between the documented rule and Cell Ranger, no read group on two references, every UB 12 bases of A, C, G and T (2.3). These are no longer assumptions about the data, only about code that is not written yet; A1, A1b and A2 test it on the first full run.
2. Applying the documented rule to the corrected UMIs of the unfiltered run is close to what a rerun would count. A rerun corrects the surviving raw UMIs again and decides low support on them (2.3). On two trial BAMs the difference is 3.6 and 42 entries per million (A6, 3.2); A6 reports it for all 24.
3. (Measured since version 2: no read group has records on two references, genome-wide, [V2]. The merge still handles and counts the case.)
4. `count` of molecule_info equals our read count genome-wide (tested on chr21 and chr22). Checked by A2.
5. (Measured since version 2: every UB of a read-group record is 12 bases of A, C, G and T, genome-wide, [V2]. The pass still stops on any other base.)
6. Keeping the alignments and the cell calls of the unfiltered run is close to a rerun. Not tested unless D8 is answered yes.
7. The scanpy stack runs with pandas 3.0.6 and numpy 2.4.6 (resolution only). Fallback: a separate venv for stages 25 and 26.
8. Run times and memory of stages 23, 24, 26 and 30 are arithmetic or extrapolations. Each is measured on a small case first.
9. Rare classes form clusters among 5,571 and 5,114 nuclei. If vascular cells form none, the paper's collapse question cannot be put to that class here, and the report says so.
10. The marker panel, taken in part from whole-cell studies, works on nuclei. The user edits it (D1) and sees the class tables at the review gate.
11. Hexamer rates of true tail-primed and true internally primed molecules do not depend on region, wherever stage 29 has to use the library-wide rates.
12. Within a stratum, labeled molecules have the templated share of unlabeled ones (the reweighted burden of stage 28).
13. The three measurements of stage 30 (BD70 chr21 and chr1, BD67 chr21) are typical of the genome: about 3% of accepted reads, and about 18% of intron sites, without a context after the 3' part placement. The full run prints both.
14. The Thermo Fisher table is the one Lexogen's ERCC module follows.
15. BD70 pigeon isoform ends, made from a trimmed BAM we have not seen, sit within 10 nt of our A-run sites.
16. The 3' v4 bead oligo design (dT length, anchor bases) is not checked and may shift sites by 1 to 2 nt (spec 11). The label uses 20 nt and is not moved by it; the position matches of stages 30 and 31 use tolerances of 10 and 5 nt, which cover it.
17. tecap runs on the whole tagged BAM of each library as it ran on chr21 (three trial runs, one contig each), and its per-site breakdown stays a manageable file genome-wide. Run on chr21 and chr22 first.
18. plotly.js loaded from cdnjs renders inside the published page.

**What would change the plan.**
- A1 or A2 fails, or A1b exceeds its gate: the resolution step of 3.2 comes first, and everything after stage 24 waits until you have seen the differing entries.
- A3 or A4 fails: the pass differs from the validated engine or from the original tool; fix before any matrix is used.
- The class tables fail the review gate: revise the panel, or take option 4 of D1 for the nuclei libraries.
- Classes are not stable across seeds before filtering: they get no collapse call and the fixed-label view stands alone for them.
- The 5 point rule fires on every axis in every library: the headline wording changes as stated in 3.6. The reweighted number still stays out of headline sentences.
- Intron sites without a context stay near 18% genome-wide: they are reported by cause. A rule for reads that splice into an unannotated exon (class by position alone, flagged as a novel last exon) is not part of this design and would be proposed to you first.
- Stage 30 is too slow per read: restrict it to reads at sites with at least 2 accepted reads, or to a seeded sample of reads per site, and say so.
- The rerun of D8 differs from our matrix by more than 1 UMI in 1,000 (`RERUN_DIFF`, our threshold): the report gives both and the impact analysis is repeated on the rerun matrix for that one setting.

**Known limits that stay.** No ambient-RNA correction and no doublet removal unless D9 is answered with option 3. 1179 counts exonic reads only. The paper's endothelial result cannot be reproduced, only asked again of our data. Thinning control, best-union Jaccard and collapse cut-offs are our design choices. The duplicate-free unit of stage 23 is a 1 kb bin, not a molecule.
